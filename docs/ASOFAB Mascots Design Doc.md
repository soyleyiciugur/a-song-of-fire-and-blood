ASOFAB Notification Mascots & Copy Design Guide

Overview

The ASOFAB website notification system uses two recurring in-universe mascots inspired by the character-driven notification approach of apps such as Duolingo.

These mascots are not merely decorative portraits. Each one has a distinct personality, vocabulary, punctuation style, and relationship with the user.

Their role is to make notifications feel like they are coming from actual inhabitants of the ASOFAB world rather than from a modern social platform.

Every notification should therefore feel:

diegetic

Westerosi or Essosi in terminology

recognizable by voice

concise enough for mobile notifications

consistent with the website's existing ASOFAB tone

readable even without knowing the mascot lore

The system currently uses two mascots:

Mara — The Stoic Tapster
Aldren — The Courtly Innkeeper

1. Core Visual Concept

Each notification uses:

the relevant mascot's mini portrait

a smaller feature icon overlaid on the portrait

a notification title

a short description written in that mascot's voice

Visual structure:

MascotMiniPortrait.webp

with a smaller notification-specific icon positioned at the bottom-right corner, similar to Duolingo's character notification treatment.

Example:

AldrenMiniPortrait.webp

Tavern tankard icon

Tavern is abuzz!
New words have been spoken in your absence, my liege.

Another example:

AldrenMiniPortrait.webp

Raven's Eye icon

New reels in the Gutter!
My liege, our little birds whisper of fresh spectacle in Flea Bottom.

The feature icon communicates where the notification came from.

The mascot communicates how the notification is being delivered.

These are separate systems.

2. Mascot One — Mara

Mara — The Stoic Tapster

Age: 24

Core personality

Mara is young, highly competent, observant, and thoroughly unimpressed by most things.

She has spent enough time serving nobles, sellswords, gamblers, loud drunks, knights, schemers, and self-important fools that very little surprises her anymore.

She is not intentionally rude.

She simply has no interest in pretending that obvious things are complicated.

Her humor comes from understatement.

She often states exactly what happened, then adds one short remark that makes it clear she has already judged the situation.

Personality keywords

dry

restrained

observant

competent

mildly sarcastic

practical

unsentimental

unimpressed

concise

Core voice principle

Westerosi language, stripped bare.

Mara must sound like someone who lives in the world of ASOIAF.

Her dryness must never turn into modern internet language.

She does not speak like a social media manager.

She speaks like a young woman behind a tavern counter who has heard every kind of nonsense imaginable.

3. Mara's Language Rules

No modern platform terminology

Mara should never casually use terms such as:

post

comment

reply

reaction

like

notification

thread

feed

DM

message

username

follower

content

Even if the underlying feature is equivalent to one of these systems, the copy should translate it into diegetic language.

Instead of:

Someone replied to your post.

Use:

Your words drew an answer
Apparently the matter was not settled.

Instead of:

Someone liked your post.

Use:

Your words found favor
Enjoy it while it lasts.

4. Mara's Punctuation

Mara should almost never use exclamation marks.

This should be treated as a strong character rule.

Her titles should generally use:

no punctuation

a full stop where appropriate

short declarative phrasing

Avoid:

The tavern is abuzz!

Prefer:

More words at the tavern

or:

The tavern carries on

Her lack of enthusiasm is part of the characterization.

Even genuinely important news should usually be delivered with restraint.

5. Mara's Humor

Mara's jokes should come from:

understatement

reluctant acknowledgement

dry observation

mild disbelief

practical cynicism

Her jokes should generally be only one short clause.

Good:

Apparently the matter was not settled.

Someone had more to say.

Enjoy it while it lasts.

The conversation continued without you. Somehow.

Someone dragged you into the matter.

Bad:

Wow! Looks like everyone loved your latest post!

Too modern, too cheerful, too explanatory.

Also bad:

Another fool has chosen to burden the realm with his worthless opinion.

Too aggressive.

Mara is unimpressed, not cruel.

7. Mascot Two — Aldren

Aldren — The Courtly Innkeeper

Age: 67

Core personality

Aldren is an elderly, highly experienced innkeeper who has spent decades dealing with nobles, courtiers, household knights, merchants, retainers, and wealthy travelers.

He knows exactly how people of rank expect to be addressed.

More importantly, he treats the user as though they are far more important than they probably are.

Aldren speaks to the player as if they were a great lord, prince, king, or queen visiting his establishment.

He uses:

my liege

Your Grace

deferential phrasing

diplomatic euphemisms

unnecessary courtesy

careful flattery

His comedy does not come from telling jokes.

It comes from being absurdly respectful about ordinary things.

8. Aldren's Personality Keywords

warm

deferential

verbose

nervous

sycophantic

old-fashioned

ceremonious

diplomatic

attentive

eager to please

Core voice principle

Westerosi language, ornamented.

Where Mara removes unnecessary words, Aldren adds them.

Where Mara says:

Someone has sent word.

Aldren might say:

A raven awaits you, my liege. Its sender seems most eager for your attention.

9. Aldren's Punctuation

Aldren may use exclamation marks.

Unlike Mara, enthusiasm suits him.

However, they should not appear in every notification.

Exclamation marks work especially well for:

good news

Tavern activity

new entertainment

celebrations

acclaim

achievements

invitations

noteworthy happenings

Example:

Tavern is abuzz!

This title belongs naturally to Aldren rather than Mara.

Aldren should still remain dignified.

He is enthusiastic because he is a courtly host, not because he is hyperactive.

10. Aldren's Humor

Aldren's humor comes from excessive deference.

He refuses to openly blame the user.

If the user is losing:

A difficult position, my liege. Through no failing of yours, naturally.

If the user asks what their own Ruler is:

You, my liege. Surely no introduction is needed.

His instinct is always:

protect the user's dignity first, explain the system second.

The occasional joke is stronger if most of her dialogue remains functional.

12. Shared Worldbuilding Rule

Both mascots speak from inside the ASOFAB world.

Neither mascot should acknowledge the website as a modern platform.

Every modern digital action should be translated into something that could plausibly be described by someone living in Westeros or Essos.

Examples:

14. Mascot Assignment Philosophy

Mascots should not be assigned randomly.

Each has natural domains.

Mara works particularly well for

replies

responses

Tavern conversation

mentions

Gutter activity

chaotic social activity

less important updates

mildly embarrassing events

routine information

situations where a dry reaction makes the notification funnier

Aldren works particularly well for

important correspondence

favor

invitations

achievements

Guild Parley

formal updates

noteworthy announcements

Raven's Eye intelligence

celebratory events

anything that can be amusingly framed as a matter of court

These are not absolute categories.

The same feature may use different mascots depending on the event.

15. Same Feature, Different Mascot

The event itself determines the voice.

A Raven's Eye notification about a grand new reel might be delivered by Aldren:

New spectacle in the Gutter!
My liege, our little birds whisper of fresh entertainment in Flea Bottom.

But a chaotic response to one of the user's reels might be Mara:

The Gutter is shouting again
Your little spectacle drew an answer.

The mascot therefore represents the tone of the event, not necessarily the application section.

16. Mascot Relationship With the User

The contrast between Mara and Aldren should become a recurring source of humor.

Aldren assumes the user is extremely important.

Mara knows perfectly well that they are not.

Aldren

The user is:

a lord

a ruler

an honored guest

someone whose dignity must be preserved

someone whose decisions surely make perfect sense

Mara

The user is:

another patron

probably involved in some nonsense

capable of understanding a short sentence

not deserving of ceremonial treatment

This contrast should never become openly hostile.

Mara is not the user's enemy.

She simply refuses to flatter them.

17. Running Dynamic

A useful internal summary:

Aldren thinks the user is the most important person in the room.

Mara has seen more important people fall asleep face-first into their ale.

Neither line necessarily needs to appear in the product.

They simply define the writing philosophy.

18. Notification Structure

Recommended standard format:

Title
Description

Titles should generally be:

short

one line where possible

immediately understandable

flavorful without becoming cryptic

Descriptions should generally:

explain the event

remain within one short sentence

occasionally use two very short sentences for comedic timing

preserve mascot personality

Avoid long lore-heavy notification copy.

The user should understand what happened immediately.

19. Voice Balance

Flavor must never obscure function.

Bad:

The winds carry murmurs from distant cups and restless souls

Beautiful, but unclear.

Better:

The tavern carries on
Several voices answered while you were away.

The user understands both the event and the tone.

22. Copy Rules — Mara

When writing Mara:

Do

keep titles restrained

avoid exclamation marks

use Westerosi vocabulary

use short sentences

let humor come from understatement

occasionally use mild sarcasm

allow some notifications to contain no joke at all

sound competent

Do not

use modern social media vocabulary

make every line sarcastic

make her openly insulting

make her sound bored to the point of apathy

use excessive medieval poetry

use enthusiastic punctuation

make her sound like a twenty-first-century teenager

Internal shorthand

Medieval deadpan.

23. Copy Rules — Aldren

When writing Aldren:

Do

use formal address

flatter the user

use courtly phrasing

occasionally overstate ordinary events

use mild ceremonial enthusiasm

protect the user's dignity

make mundane events sound faintly important

Do not

make him stupid

make him cartoonishly submissive

overload every sentence with titles

use three exclamation marks

turn him into a generic butler

make every notification extremely long

Internal shorthand

A tavern keeper who treats every patron like visiting royalty.

25. Important Writing Principle

The mascot's personality should mostly appear in the description, not through excessively clever titles.

Titles need to remain functional.

This is especially important for Mara.

A user should be able to glance at the notification and understand what happened.

Flavor follows clarity.

26. Consistency Rule

Before approving any mascot notification, check four things.

Does it sound like someone from Westeros or Essos?

If the wording contains obvious modern product terminology, rewrite it.

Is the event immediately understandable?

Worldbuilding should not make the notification cryptic.

Could the other mascot have said the exact same line?

If yes, the voice probably needs more personality.

Is the joke stronger than the information?

If yes, simplify it.

Role: The Stoic Tapster

Punctuation:

Aldren

Name: Aldren

28. Core Design Contrast

The entire mascot system can be reduced to one distinction:

Aldren dresses the information up.

Mara strips the information down.

Both remain completely inside the same Westerosi world.

That contrast should remain recognizable across notifications, tooltips, The Great Game, Raven's Eye, Tavern activity, Guild Parley, and any future ASOFAB social systems.
